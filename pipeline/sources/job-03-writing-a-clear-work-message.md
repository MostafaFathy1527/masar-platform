# Source note — Writing a clear work message or email

Author's own note, written from public knowledge of workplace writing. No employer material,
no named companies, no statistics. The only permitted input for lesson JOB-03.

Objective: **JOB-03** — given a situation that needs a written message at work, write one whose
first line states the purpose and whose reader knows exactly what is being asked and by when.

Arabic register: Modern Standard Arabic throughout the Arabic fields.

---

A work message is read by someone busy, often on a phone, often between other tasks. A message
that makes the reader work out what it is about gets a late reply, a wrong reply, or none.

**Purpose first.** The first line says why you are writing: a request, an update, a question, a
decision needed. Background comes after, and only as much as the reader needs to act.

**One clear ask.** If you need something, say exactly what, from whom, and by when. "Can you
look at this?" leaves the reader to guess what "look at" means. "Can you confirm by Thursday
whether the figures in section two are final?" does not. If there are several requests, number
them, so the reply can answer each one.

**A subject line that says what is inside.** For email, the subject is often the only part read
when someone searches later. "Question" is useless; "Delivery date for the March order — need
your confirmation" is findable.

**Short paragraphs, plain words.** One idea per paragraph. Avoid long introductions and
repeated apologies. Polite and direct are not opposites: a greeting, a clear request and a
thank-you is both.

**Tone.** Written words lose the tone of voice that softens speech, so a short message can read
as cold or angry. Read it once as the receiver would. Do not write when angry; save the draft
and return to it.

**Before sending.** Is the right person addressed, and does everyone copied need to be? Are
attachments actually attached? Are names, dates and numbers correct? Would the reader know what
to do if this were the only message they read today?

**Replying.** Answer every question that was asked, in the same order. If you cannot answer yet,
say so and say when you will.

A common mistake is putting the actual request in the last line of a long message, where it is
missed. Another is sending several short messages instead of one complete one.
