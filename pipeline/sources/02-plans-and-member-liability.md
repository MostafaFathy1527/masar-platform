# Source note — Plans, benefits and member liability

Author's own note, written from public knowledge of how insurance cost-sharing works.
Payer-agnostic. No client or employer material. This is the only permitted input for
generating lesson 02.

Objectives: MI-02 (distinguish copay / deductible / coinsurance / out-of-pocket maximum,
and compute member liability), MI-03 (read a benefit schedule to decide covered /
excluded / limited / pre-authorisation required).

---

Insurance rarely pays everything. What the member owes is decided by a small set of
mechanisms that behave differently from one another, and most confusion at a front desk
comes from treating them as one thing called "the patient's share".

A **copay** is a fixed amount for a defined service — a flat figure, known in advance,
that does not change with the cost of the encounter. It is the easiest to explain and the
easiest to collect, which is why it is the one people recognise.

A **deductible** is an amount the member pays before the policy begins to share cost at
all. It accumulates across the policy year. A member early in the year may be told their
service is covered and still owe the full amount, because covered and payable are not the
same thing — the coverage exists, but the deductible has not been met.

**Coinsurance** is a percentage of the allowed amount, applied after the deductible is
met. Because it is a percentage, the member cannot know the figure until the payer has
priced the service. This is the mechanism that most often produces a bill the member did
not expect, and it is worth saying so plainly rather than implying every amount can be
quoted in advance.

The **out-of-pocket maximum** is a ceiling. Once the member's own spending reaches it, the
policy pays the remainder for the rest of the year. Premiums do not count toward it, which
surprises people; the premium buys the policy, it is not spending under it.

The order matters and is worth memorising: deductible first, then coinsurance, and both
are bounded by the out-of-pocket maximum. A copay may sit outside that sequence
altogether depending on the plan.

A worked example, using training figures only. An encounter is allowed at 4,800. The
member has 1,000 of deductible remaining and 20% coinsurance. They pay the 1,000, then 20%
of the remaining 3,800, which is 760. Their liability is 1,760 and the payer pays 3,040 —
unless the 1,760 would carry them past their out-of-pocket maximum, in which case the
ceiling applies and the payer pays more.

The second half of the objective is reading a benefit schedule. A schedule assigns each
category of service to one of four states, and the practical skill is deciding which
applies before the service rather than after.

**Covered** means the policy pays subject to the usual cost sharing. **Excluded** means
the policy does not pay at all, and no amount of correct paperwork changes that — an
excluded service is a self-pay conversation to have before delivery, not an appeal to file
afterwards. **Limited** means covered up to a stated cap: a number of visits, a monetary
limit, or a period. The cap is the thing to check, because a member who has used their
limit looks identical on the card to one who has not. **Pre-authorisation required** means
the policy will pay, but only if permission was obtained first.

The most expensive misreading is treating "limited" as "covered". The service is genuinely
in the schedule, the member is genuinely eligible, and the claim is still refused because
the limit was already reached. Nothing about the card or the eligibility response shows
this; it requires checking the accumulated use.

A note on language for anyone at a desk: "covered" is the word members hear as "free". It
is worth never using it alone. "Covered, and your share will be X" is a different sentence
from "covered", and it prevents most of the arguments that follow a bill.
