# Source note — Your first week: learning the job on purpose

Author's own note, written from public knowledge of starting a new job. No employer material,
no named companies, no statistics. The only permitted input for lesson JOB-04.

Objective: **JOB-04** — given a new-starter situation, choose the questions and actions that
establish what is expected, who to ask, and how work is checked, within the first week.

Arabic register: Modern Standard Arabic throughout the Arabic fields.

---

The first week is when it is easiest to ask questions and hardest to know which ones to ask.
People who start well are usually not the ones who already know everything, but the ones who
find out deliberately what matters.

**Find out what "doing this job well" means here.** Ask your manager directly: what does a good
first month look like? Which tasks matter most? How will I know if something I did is wrong?
Expectations that are never said cannot be met on purpose.

**Learn who to ask for what.** Most workplaces have a person who knows the systems, a person who
knows the customers, a person who approves things. Knowing who they are saves days of guessing.
Write names and what each person helps with.

**Write things down.** New information arrives faster than it can be remembered: passwords and
access, procedures, names, where files are kept. A personal notebook of "how we do X here" means
you ask each question once.

**Ask good questions.** Before asking, try for a few minutes to find the answer yourself, then
ask, saying what you already tried. "I looked in the shared folder and the guide, and could not
find the price list — where is it kept?" respects the other person's time and shows effort.
Group small questions instead of interrupting for each one.

**Watch how things are actually done.** Written procedures and real practice often differ. Notice
how colleagues communicate, how quickly people reply, how decisions are made. Copy what works
before suggesting changes.

**Be careful with changes early.** New people often see problems clearly. Note them and raise them
after you understand why things are done the way they are; there may be a reason you have not
seen yet.

**Agree a check-in.** A short regular conversation with your manager in the first weeks catches
misunderstandings while they are small.

A common mistake is staying silent to look capable, then discovering weeks later that a task was
being done wrongly the whole time.
