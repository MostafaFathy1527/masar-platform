# Source note — Writing a CV that gets read

Author's own note, written from public knowledge of how CVs are read in hiring. No employer
material, no named companies, no statistics. The only permitted input for lesson JOB-01.

Objective: **JOB-01** — given a short job advert and a draft CV, rewrite the CV's entries so
each one shows a result relevant to the advert, and remove what does not help.

Arabic register: Modern Standard Arabic throughout the Arabic fields.

---

A CV is read quickly. The first reader is often scanning many CVs for one thing: does this
person look able to do this particular job? A CV that makes the reader search for the answer
usually loses to one that puts it near the top.

**Start from the advert, not from your history.** Read the advert and underline what it asks
for: the tasks, the skills, the tools. Then choose what to put in the CV, and in what order, by
how directly it answers those lines. The same person sends different CVs to different jobs;
that is not dishonest, it is selection.

**An entry is a result, not a duty.** "Responsible for customer calls" says what the job was.
"Answered about forty customer calls a day and resolved most of them without passing them on"
says what the person did and how well. A strong entry has three parts: an action verb, what was
done, and what changed because of it. If an exact number is not known, do not invent one; a
plain, true description of the outcome is better than a precise false one.

**Experience includes more than paid jobs.** For a first job, volunteering, a school or
university project, helping in a family business, organising an event, or teaching yourself a
tool all count, written the same way: what you did and what came of it.

**Structure.** Name and a way to be contacted at the top. A short line or two saying what kind
of work you are looking for and what you bring, matched to the advert. Then experience (newest
first), then education, then skills that the advert actually asks about. One to two pages for
an early career.

**What to remove.** Personal details the job does not need, unless the advert or local practice
asks for them. Long lists of soft skills with no evidence ("hard-working, team player") — the
entries should show these instead. Anything untrue: a CV is checked at the interview, and a
claim that cannot be explained is worse than no claim.

**Check before sending.** Spelling and names correct, dates consistent, the same font and
layout throughout, a file name that includes your name, and the format the advert asked for.

A common mistake is writing one CV and sending it everywhere. Another is copying the advert's
words without any evidence behind them; the reader notices words that are not supported by an
entry.
