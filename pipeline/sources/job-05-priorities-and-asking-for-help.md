# Source note — Priorities, deadlines and asking for help early

Author's own note, written from public knowledge of managing workload. No employer material,
no named companies, no statistics. The only permitted input for lesson JOB-05.

Objective: **JOB-05** — given several tasks with deadlines and a risk of missing one, order the
work and tell the right person early, with the facts and a proposed solution.

Arabic register: Modern Standard Arabic throughout the Arabic fields.

---

At work there is usually more to do than time to do it. The skill is not doing everything; it is
deciding what comes first and saying early when something will not fit.

**List before deciding.** Write every task down with its deadline and who is waiting for it. Work
held only in your head gets forgotten or underestimated.

**Urgent and important are different questions.** Urgent means it has a near deadline. Important
means it matters a lot if it is wrong or missing. A simple way to sort: do first what is both;
plan time for what is important but not urgent; handle quickly or hand back what is urgent but
not important; question whether the rest needs doing at all. When it is unclear what matters
more, ask the person who set the work instead of guessing.

**Estimate honestly.** Tasks usually take longer than the first guess, especially ones you have
not done before. Add time for checking and for interruptions.

**Raise a problem as soon as you see it.** A deadline that will be missed is a fact; the only
choice is when people learn it. Telling your manager two days before gives them options: more
time, more help, a smaller version, a different priority. Telling them on the day gives them
none.

**How to raise it.** Say what the task is, what the problem is, what you have already done, and
what you propose. "The report is due Thursday. The data from the other team has not arrived, and
without it I cannot finish section three. I have asked them twice. I can send sections one and
two on Thursday and section three a day after the data arrives — does that work?" This is
asking for help without handing over the whole problem.

**Agree, then confirm.** When priorities change, confirm the new order in writing, briefly, so
everyone has the same understanding.

A common mistake is working late in silence to hide a problem, then failing anyway. Another is
saying yes to every new request without saying what it will delay.
